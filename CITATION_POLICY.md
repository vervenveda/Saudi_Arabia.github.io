# Saudi Arabia Gateway — Citation Policy

Updated: 2026-10-24

## Purpose

This repository separates **what a source directly establishes** from broader interpretation. Citations should appear close enough to a claim that a reader can determine which evidence supports it.

## Source hierarchy

1. **Primary / item-level evidence** — inscriptions, archaeological records, historical documents, maps, photographs, official decrees, contemporaneous newspapers, recorded performances, and datasets.
2. **Scholarly synthesis** — peer-reviewed research, academic monographs, museum scholarship, archaeological publications, critical editions and reputable reference works.
3. **Institutional heritage records** — UNESCO and comparable bodies. These are strong sources for the institution's findings, inscription status, criteria, and descriptions.
4. **Government / state-affiliated sources** — useful for laws, official chronology, programs, policy goals, statistics and institutional positions. They are not automatically independent corroboration of contested or evaluative claims.
5. **Journalism and civil-society reporting** — useful for dated contemporary evidence when provenance, methodology and editorial standards are clear.

## Historical naming rule

Do not project the modern Kingdom of Saudi Arabia unchanged into earlier periods. Prefer historically precise wording such as:

- “the Hail rock-art landscape in the geography of present-day Saudi Arabia”;
- “the Nabataean site of Hegra in northwestern Arabia”;
- “At-Turaif in Diriyah, centre of the First Saudi State”;
- “the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, proclaimed in 1932.”

## Founding chronology rule

The repository may discuss both:
- **1727**, the date used by the modern Saudi state for the beginning of Imam Muhammad bin Saud’s rule and the First Saudi State; and
- **1744**, a date widely used in earlier scholarship for the alliance between Muhammad bin Saud and Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab.

Do not silently treat one convention as if the other never existed. State the source, the event being dated, and the chronology being used.

## Living heritage rule

For Al-Qatt Al-Asiri, Al-Sadu, Ardah, Almezmar, Semsemiah and other living practices:
- identify the communities and regions connected to the practice;
- distinguish documentation of the practice from decorative imitation;
- do not present original CSS studies or classroom exercises as authentic heritage artifacts;
- avoid implying that all Saudis practice a regional tradition.

## Contemporary claims

Every current statistic, policy milestone, development claim, legal rule or human-rights claim should carry:
- a date or reporting period;
- a source;
- the institution responsible for the claim or measurement;
- independent corroboration where the claim is disputed or evaluative;
- a clear distinction among announced goal, implemented action, measured result and opinion.

## Images and media

Record creator/archive, item title, date where known, source URL, license or rights basis, and whether the image is documentary, archival, reconstructed or illustrative. Never imply that an old photograph depicts a current condition.

## Quotations

Use short quotations only when the exact wording matters. Prefer paraphrase plus citation. Verify translations against a reliable edition when the wording affects interpretation.

## Correction standard

Substantive corrections should be recorded in the repository update log or commit history. Do not silently rewrite a contested claim in a way that erases the earlier error.
