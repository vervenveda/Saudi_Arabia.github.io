# Evaluating Sources for Saudi Arabia Research

## 1. Identify the source type

Ask whether you are reading an artifact record, inscription, archaeological report, government document, official statistic, heritage nomination, memoir, academic study, newspaper report, oral history, photograph, performance record or later summary.

Different sources can answer different questions.

## 2. Ask what the source can establish

Examples:

- A UNESCO World Heritage page can establish what UNESCO inscribed, its criteria and the evidence summarized in that record.
- A Saudi ministry page can establish the ministry's stated chronology, policy or program.
- A historical photograph can establish that a photographed scene existed at a particular time and place if the cataloguing is reliable.
- A petroglyph can provide material evidence of imagery and human activity, but does not by itself reveal the artist's exact identity, language or political affiliation.
- A living-heritage inventory can document a practice and community, but should not be treated as proof that every resident of a region practices it.

## 3. Separate institution from independence

A source can be authoritative for its own act without being independent.

For example, a royal decree is primary evidence that a decree was issued. A government explainer is useful for the state's interpretation of that decree. Independent scholarship is still needed for broader evaluation of causes, consequences or contested historical meaning.

## 4. Date the claim

For archaeology, ask the dating method and range.  
For history, distinguish event date from later commemoration.  
For contemporary data, record the reporting period and access date.

## 5. Corroborate

For important claims, seek another source that does not merely repeat the first. Prefer different evidence types when possible: an archaeological record plus scholarship, a policy document plus independent outcome data, or a heritage record plus item-level documentation.

## 6. Check translation and transliteration

Arabic names can appear in multiple English spellings: Hegra/al-Hijr/Madā’in Ṣāliḥ; Diriyah/ad-Dir'iyah; Al-Ahsa/al-Aḥsā’. Record variants when they affect search or citation.

## 7. Record uncertainty

Use labels such as:

- **Documented**
- **Well-supported**
- **Contested**
- **Source-specific**
- **Provisional**
- **Unverified**
- **Corrected**

Uncertainty is part of scholarship, not a failure of scholarship.
