RESEARCH CHAPTER 02

Hegra & Ancient Networks

Hegra is evidence of Nabataean architecture, inscription, water management and long-distance connection in northwestern Arabia. Study it as a Nabataean site in the geography of present-day Saudi Arabia—not as the modern state projected backward.

Investigate in the Notebook →Primary sources ↗
Observe first. Interpret second. State the limitation.
01 / MONUMENTAL STONE

Tombs carved into sandstone

UNESCO records 111 monumental tombs at Hegra, 94 with decorated façades, mainly dating from the first century BCE through the first century CE. Their form connects architecture, craft, status and landscape.

Façade

Observe carved architectural elements, scale and relation to the rock face before comparing stylistic influence.

Inscription

Inscriptions can provide names, formulas, dates or legal information when read through specialist editions.

Water

Wells and hydraulic features are evidence for settlement, engineering and adaptation—not just decoration around tombs.

02 / NETWORKS

Northwestern Arabia was connected, not isolated

The Nabataean world linked routes, settlements and markets across Arabia and the Levant. Hegra’s architecture and inscriptions can be studied as evidence of those connections without assuming that every stylistic similarity proves direct migration or political control.

Trade evidence

Look for routes, commodities, inscriptions, settlement patterns and comparative archaeology.

Cultural exchange

Shared forms can reflect exchange, adaptation, prestige or workshop practice; the explanation must be argued.

Political labels

Use “Nabataean Hegra” for the relevant period rather than “ancient Saudi kingdom.”

03 / ACADEMY INQUIRY

Compare tomb, inscription and water evidence

Build a claim about what Hegra reveals about Nabataean society. Require three evidence types and include one thing the evidence cannot tell you with confidence.
