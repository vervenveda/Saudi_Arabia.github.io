RESEARCH CHAPTER 07

Built Landscapes & Water

Architecture in Saudi Arabia is inseparable from climate, water, material and social organization. Compare clay, coral, wood, stone and oasis infrastructure as different regional responses.

Investigate in the Notebook →Primary sources ↗
Observe first. Interpret second. State the limitation.
01 / NAJD & HIJAZ

Different climates, different buildings

At-Turaif demonstrates Najdi adobe construction and geometric decoration adapted to central Arabia. Historic Jeddah developed coral masonry and large wooden roshan in a hot, humid Red Sea port shaped by commerce and pilgrimage.

Thermal mass

Thick earthen walls can moderate temperature swings; test the principle rather than assuming every building performs identically.

Ventilation

Roshan and openings can be studied for light, airflow, privacy and façade performance.

Material supply

Local material availability, trade and craft networks shape what can be built and maintained.

02 / AL-AHSA

The oasis is infrastructure

UNESCO describes Al-Ahsa as a cultural landscape of gardens, canals, springs, wells, drainage, historic buildings and settlement. Architecture here includes the management of water and agriculture, not only individual monuments.

Water network

Map source, conveyance, field, settlement and drainage to understand the system.

Cultural landscape

A landscape can be heritage because relationships among natural and human-made elements persist over time.

Current condition

Historical water systems do not tell you today’s water balance. Use current hydrological and agricultural data for present-day claims.

03 / ACADEMY INQUIRY

Design from constraints

Choose Najd, Jeddah, Asir or Al-Ahsa. List climate, material, water and social constraints, then identify architectural responses supported by evidence. Create a diagram but label any proposed design as your own interpretation.
