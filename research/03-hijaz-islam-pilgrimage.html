RESEARCH CHAPTER 03

Hijaz, Islam & Pilgrimage

Western Arabia became central to Muslim religious history, while pilgrimage and Red Sea exchange linked Mecca, Medina and Jeddah to communities far beyond the peninsula.

Investigate in the Notebook →Primary sources ↗
Observe first. Interpret second. State the limitation.
01 / PLACE & RELIGION

Mecca and Medina in Muslim life

Mecca and Medina have distinct religious significance within Islam. Historical research should distinguish devotional meaning, legal rules of access, pilgrimage practice, urban history and archaeological questions rather than treating them as one category.

Devotional source

Useful for understanding belief, ritual and religious meaning from within a tradition.

Historical source

Useful for chronology, institutions, travel, settlement or change over time.

Do not substitute

A devotional text and an archaeological report answer different questions; neither should be forced to do the other’s job.

02 / RED SEA GATEWAY

Jeddah: trade, pilgrimage and cosmopolitan urbanism

UNESCO describes Historic Jeddah as a major Red Sea trading and pilgrimage city. Coral masonry, woodwork and roshan tower houses reflect climate, commerce and the accommodation of pilgrims arriving by sea.

Architecture

Roshan façades can be studied for ventilation, privacy, light and street relationship.

Mobility

Pilgrims, merchants and sailors made the Hijaz part of wider Indian Ocean and Red Sea networks.

Social history

A heritage building can reveal urban form; social diversity requires additional records about people, work, family and migration.

03 / ACADEMY INQUIRY

Trace one route into the Hijaz

Choose a pilgrimage or Red Sea connection. Map origin, route, transport, destination and one cultural exchange. Distinguish evidence for movement from assumptions about identity.
