RESEARCH CHAPTER 04

Diriyah & Saudi State Formation

Diriyah anchors the history of early Saudi political power, but chronology depends on which event a source chooses to date. This chapter keeps 1727, 1744 and later state phases distinct.

Observe first. Interpret second. State the limitation.
01 / DIRIYAH & AT-TURAIF

Architecture and political history meet

UNESCO identifies At-Turaif as the first capital of the Saudi dynasty and an outstanding Najdi urban and architectural ensemble. Its adobe construction and geometric decoration also provide evidence about environmental adaptation in central Arabia.

1727

The modern Saudi state uses 1727 for the beginning of Imam Muhammad bin Saud’s rule and Founding Day chronology.

1744

Earlier scholarship commonly used 1744 for the alliance between Muhammad bin Saud and Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab as a pivotal state-forming milestone.

Research rule

Ask “what event is this date marking?” before asking which date is correct.

02 / FIRST & SECOND STATES

Political continuity and disruption

The First Saudi State ended in 1818 after the Egyptian-Ottoman campaign. A Saudi state centred on Riyadh was re-established under Turki bin Abdullah in 1824 and continued, amid conflict and changing power, until 1891.

Continuity

Dynastic connection, political memory and institutions can be studied across phases.

Disruption

Military defeat, shifts of capital, rival powers and internal conflict mean the phases were not one uninterrupted administration.

Source perspective

Official histories, foreign accounts and later scholarship may emphasize different causes and turning points.
