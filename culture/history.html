03 / HISTORY THROUGH THE AGES

Layers of Time

The history of present-day Saudi Arabia is deeper than the modern state: prehistoric landscapes, caravan and oasis networks, Nabataean Hegra, the rise of Islam, pilgrimage routes, regional polities, Saudi states, oil-era transformation and a rapidly changing present.

01 / CHRONOLOGY

A long history before 1932

Political borders and identities changed repeatedly. This timeline uses archaeological and documentary anchors without projecting the modern nation-state unchanged into the distant past.

PREHISTORY & EARLY SETTLEMENT

Changing climates, movement and rock art

Hail’s rock-art landscapes preserve human and animal imagery across thousands of years and changing environments.

ANCIENT OASES & TRADE

Northwestern Arabia in wider networks

Oasis settlements and caravan routes connected Arabia with the Levant, Mesopotamia, the Red Sea and beyond. Hegra became a major Nabataean site with monumental tombs.

7TH CENTURY & AFTER

Islam, pilgrimage and connected cities

Islam emerged in western Arabia; Mecca and Medina became central to Muslim religious life, while pilgrimage routes connected the Hijaz to communities across Africa and Asia.

15TH–19TH CENTURIES

Diriyah, Najdi state formation and Red Sea trade

At-Turaif in Diriyah developed as the first capital of the Saudi dynasty; meanwhile Historic Jeddah’s trading and pilgrimage roles produced a distinctive multicultural Red Sea city.

1932

Proclamation of the modern Kingdom

The Kingdom of Saudi Arabia was formally proclaimed in 1932 under Abdulaziz Al Saud after a long process of conquest, alliance and political consolidation.

1938 ONWARD

Oil and accelerated transformation

Commercial oil transformed public finance, infrastructure, cities, migration, education and Saudi Arabia’s international relationships.

21ST CENTURY

Heritage, diversification and debate

Large-scale development, tourism, cultural investment and economic diversification are reshaping public space while raising questions about conservation, sustainability, rights and social change.

02 / FOUR EVIDENCE LENSES

How do we know?

Archaeology

Sites, material remains, landscape evidence, dating and inscriptions help reconstruct societies without relying only on later narrative.

Written sources

Chronicles, administrative documents, travel accounts and inscriptions must be read for authorship, audience, date and purpose.

Built heritage

Diriyah, Jeddah, Hegra, Al-Ahsa and other places preserve evidence of environment, trade, authority, craft and daily life.

Living memory

Oral traditions and family memory matter, but should be identified as memory and compared with other evidence when making historical claims.
