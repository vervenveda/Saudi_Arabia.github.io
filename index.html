03 / CULTURE & HERITAGE

Traditions that continue to evolve

Saudi society is diverse across regions, generations, and communities. Its cultural life includes long-standing traditions as well as contemporary art, design, music, literature, sport, and entrepreneurship.

Hospitality

Welcoming guests, sharing Arabic coffee and dates, and offering generosity are widely recognized expressions of hospitality, with customs varying by place and family.

Language & expression

Arabic carries a rich literary and poetic heritage. Regional dialects, storytelling, calligraphy, textiles, music, and contemporary creative work all contribute to a living culture.

Faith & pilgrimage

Mecca and Medina are Islam’s two holiest cities and destinations for Muslim pilgrimage. Religious traditions are central to the Kingdom’s identity and global connections.

Food & gathering

Shared meals and coffee traditions express welcome and belonging. Cuisine varies regionally and reflects local ingredients, trade, climate, and family traditions.

Living heritage

Historic districts, archaeological landscapes, craft traditions, oral histories, and UNESCO-recognized heritage connect the past with present-day communities.

Contemporary creativity

Artists, filmmakers, designers, writers, athletes, and entrepreneurs are shaping new cultural spaces while engaging with heritage and global influences.

04 / A SHORT HISTORY

History is a long conversation

This compact timeline highlights a few milestones. It does not represent the full history of the many societies that have lived across the peninsula.

Ancient eras

Trade routes, oasis communities, inscriptions, and archaeological sites show the peninsula’s long connections with neighboring regions.

7th century

Islam emerged in western Arabia, and Mecca and Medina became central places in the religious history of the Muslim world.

1727 / 1744

Different historical conventions identify 1727 with the founding of the First Saudi State and 1744 with a pivotal political-religious alliance.

1932

The modern Kingdom of Saudi Arabia was formally unified and proclaimed under Abdulaziz Al Saud.

1938 onward

Commercial oil production transformed the economy, infrastructure, public services, and the Kingdom’s global relationships.

2016 onward

Vision 2030 set out a national agenda for economic diversification, social development, and expanded cultural and tourism sectors.

Historical interpretations may differ. For deeper study, compare museum and archaeological scholarship, academic histories, primary documents, and regional perspectives.

05 / SAUDI ARABIA TODAY

A country in transition

Saudi Arabia is pursuing economic diversification in tourism, logistics, technology, entertainment, manufacturing, and energy. The scale and effects of these changes should be assessed using evidence, including outcomes for people and the environment.

  • Economic transformationNew industries, private-sector growth, investment, and the challenge of building resilient livelihoods.
  • Education and opportunitySkills, research, employment, entrepreneurship, and quality of life for citizens and residents.
  • Water and climate resilienceWater security, desert ecosystems, biodiversity, energy transition, and sustainable urban development.
  • Rights and public accountabilityUnderstanding policy claims alongside independent reporting, lived experience, and credible documentation.

Learn with curiosity. Evaluate with care.

Official plans explain government priorities; independent research and people’s experiences help assess outcomes. When a subject is contested, consult multiple credible sources and distinguish established facts from projections and opinion. Respect for people and honest scrutiny can coexist.

06 / PROPOSED FRAMEWORK

A partnership measured by outcomes

INDEPENDENT PROPOSAL · NOT AN EXISTING AGREEMENT

The U.S.–Saudi relationship includes trade, investment, energy, security, education, and regional diplomacy. This independent proposal outlines principles for cooperation where interests align, clear safeguards where risks exist, and public accountability for commitments. It is a proposal—not an existing bilateral agreement.

For official services, laws, and government information

Mutual prosperity & economic fairness

Promote responsible investment, fair competition, small-business opportunity, skilled employment, and transparent economic commitments.

Security without blank checks

Support lawful defensive cooperation, diplomacy, crisis prevention, civilian protection, and meaningful oversight. Avoid automatic commitments to conflict.

Human dignity & mutual hospitality

Build education and cultural exchange, encourage respectful dialogue, and uphold human dignity without pretending that disagreements do not exist.

Nonproliferation & regional peace

Support peaceful nuclear energy only with robust safeguards and verification; advance de-escalation, humanitarian relief, and regional diplomacy.

Public reporting

Publish accessible progress updates, explain commitments and spending, identify responsible agencies, and document both results and shortcomings.

Regional cooperation

Encourage practical diplomacy across the Gulf and wider region on water, food security, public health, environmental resilience, and conflict prevention.
