01 / The land

A vast and varied landscape

Saudi Arabia occupies most of the Arabian Peninsula. Desert landscapes are central to its geography, alongside mountain ranges, coastal regions, oases, and growing cities.

01

Desert & open country

From the immense Rubʿ al Khali, or Empty Quarter, to rocky plateaus and dunes, the landscape holds deep ecological and cultural significance.

02

Coasts & mountains

The Red Sea and Arabian Gulf shape coastal life, trade, and ecosystems. The southwest includes highlands with distinctive climates and landscapes.

03

Historic places

Oases, pilgrimage routes, old ports, and settlements reflect centuries of travel, exchange, scholarship, and regional connections.

02 / Culture & heritage

Traditions that continue to evolve

Saudi society is diverse across regions, generations, and communities. Its cultural life includes long-standing traditions as well as contemporary art, design, music, literature, sport, and entrepreneurship.

Hospitality

Welcoming guests, sharing coffee and dates, and offering generosity are widely recognized expressions of hospitality, with customs varying by place and family.

Language & expression

Arabic carries a rich literary and poetic heritage. Regional dialects, storytelling, calligraphy, and contemporary creative work contribute to a living cultural landscape.

History & faith

Mecca and Medina are Islam’s two holiest cities and destinations for Muslim pilgrimage. The region’s wider history also includes ancient trade routes and diverse communities.

03 / The present

A country in transition

Saudi Arabia is seeking to diversify its economy and expand activity in areas such as tourism, logistics, technology, entertainment, manufacturing, and renewable energy. These changes bring opportunities and difficult questions about jobs, affordability, sustainability, social change, and public accountability.

Understanding the country means looking beyond a single narrative: official ambitions, lived experiences, independent evidence, and differing perspectives all matter.

  • Economic transformationEfforts to broaden economic activity beyond oil and develop new industries.
  • People and opportunityEducation, employment, entrepreneurship, housing, and quality of life.
  • Environment and waterArid-climate resilience, water security, biodiversity, and energy transition.

Learn with curiosity. Evaluate with care.

Separate documented facts from projections and opinion. When a subject is contested, consult multiple credible sources—including local voices, independent research, and primary documents. Respect for people and honest scrutiny can exist together.

04 / International relationships

Cooperation with clear principles

The relationship between Saudi Arabia and the United States includes trade, investment, energy, security, education, and regional diplomacy. A durable partnership should be judged by its outcomes for people—not simply by announcements or headline investment figures.

Shared prosperity

Encourage fair trade, responsible investment, small-business opportunity, skilled employment, and safeguards against corruption and favoritism.

Security & peace

Support diplomacy, crisis prevention, civilian protection, lawful defensive cooperation, and meaningful oversight of major commitments.

Dignity & accountability

Expand cultural and educational exchange, encourage respectful dialogue, protect human dignity, and measure commitments through credible public evidence.

Guiding idea: Countries need not agree on everything to treat people fairly, communicate honestly, and work toward a more peaceful future.
